## I build AI agents. Then I make them prove their work.

I'm David. Most of what fills an engineer's week was never thinking in the first place: the tickets, the reviews, the jobs nobody was hired to do. I build agents that take that work, and I build the guardrails that keep a person in charge of what ships.

By day I'm a Senior Backend Engineer at Aeroflow Health, building healthcare agents on Amazon Bedrock and Strands and the integrations behind them. Before that I spent years in healthcare software, and before *that* I was in QA. I broke software for a living long before I built it. That's why I don't trust anything that can't show its evidence, including my own approval on the fifth review of the day.

### The rules I build by

1. **Agents do the work. People merge.** Every run ends in a pull request. The agent never merges its own change.
2. **Deny by default.** An allowlist of tools, an isolated worktree, none of the secrets. Anything not granted is refused.
3. **Evidence over confidence.** A finding has to quote the line it's about. If the quote isn't in the diff, the finding gets thrown out.

### What I'm building

- **[health-types](https://github.com/dmhalejr/health-types)**: TypeScript types for HL7 FHIR (STU3, R4, R4B, R5), generated from HL7's own definitions. Types only. No runtime, no dependencies.
- **[git-greener](https://github.com/dmhalejr/greener)**: The grass is always greener on the other side, so bring your commits with you. Replays your commit history with the dates intact. Metadata only, no source code.
- **[Timberclove](https://timberclove.com)**: Registration for homeschool co-ops. A fair turn in line, seats held while you pay, teachers paid directly.
- **More Than Words**: An AI-powered AAC board generator. Communication is a human right, not a premium feature. In development.
